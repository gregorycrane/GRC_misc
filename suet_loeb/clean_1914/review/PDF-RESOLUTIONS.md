# Wording findings

## Augustus 51.1

1914: **Not to give the full list of the men of the opposite faction whom he not only pardoned and spared, but allowed to hold high positions in the state**. The 1914 PDF agrees with the supplied 1970 OCR against the recast legacy XML. The XML discrepancy is not explained by these two printing dates; the cause of the alteration is not established. [PDF page 235](../pages/p0235.jpg).

## Augustus 53.3

1914: **resolved to end his life by starvation**. Both supplied printings support this wording, not the legacy XML's starve himself to death. [PDF page 239](../pages/p0239.jpg).

## Augustus 60.1

1914: **contribute the funds for finishing the temple of Jupiter Olympius**. Both supplied printings support this wording; the recast legacy XML is not used as the base. [PDF page 247](../pages/p0247.jpg).

## Augustus 61.1

1914: **Now that I have shown how he conducted himself in civil and military positions, and in ruling the State in all parts of the world in peace and in war**. Both supplied printings support this wording; retain the printed sentence rather than the recast legacy version. [PDF page 247](../pages/p0247.jpg).

## Caligula 18.2

1914: **gifts**. Genuine printing difference: the 1914 PDF reads gifts; the 1970 OCR reads gift-tokens. Use gifts in the 1914 base. [PDF page 461](../pages/p0461.jpg).

## Caligula 50.1

1914: **a huge body**. Genuine printing difference: the 1970 OCR reads an unshapely body. Use a huge body in the 1914 base. [PDF page 511](../pages/p0511.jpg).

## Caligula 58.2

1914: **Do your duty,**. Genuine printing difference: the 1970 OCR reads Take that. The legacy wording Do your duty is supported by this 1914 scan. [PDF page 525](../pages/p0525.jpg).

## Tiberius 14.4

1914: **as an adept in the art**. Genuine printing difference: the 1970 OCR reads a learned man. Use the 1914 wording. [PDF page 347](../pages/p0347.jpg).

## Tiberius 42.2

1914: **He established a new office**. The PDF has He, not the later Finally he. The legacy 1951 annotation documents the alternative; it is not Gemini translation. [PDF page 383](../pages/p0383.jpg).

## Tiberius 48.2

1914: **a hundred denarii**. The 1914 PDF reads a hundred; the 1970 OCR reads a thousand. The 1914 reading is retained without emendation from the Latin. [PDF page 389](../pages/p0389.jpg).

## Tiberius 61.1

1914: **grandsons and grand-daughter**. Genuine printing difference: the 1970 OCR reads daughter-in-law. The legacy grand-daughter is attested in 1914. [PDF page 407](../pages/p0407.jpg).

## Tiberius 43–47

1914: **XLV. How grossly he was in the habit of**. Chapters 43–44 are Latin in the English column; 45–47 already have English translation in 1914. Use Gemini supplements only for 43–44, retaining inherited 45–47 separately as archival alternatives. [PDF page 385](../pages/p0385.jpg).
